Choosing who renovates your home is one of the most important decisions in the whole project. The right person makes the process calmer and the result better. The wrong fit can cost money, time and a lot of stress. Here's what we'd look for.

## Do they listen before they sell?

A good first conversation is mostly about your home and what you want — not a pitch. Notice whether the person asks how you use the space, what's frustrating you and what you'd like the finished room to feel like. If they jump straight to a price or a standard solution, they may not be thinking about your house specifically.

## Are they honest about trade-offs?

Every renovation involves compromises: budget, layout, materials, timing. A trustworthy carpenter will tell you when something is likely to be impractical, where spending more will make a real difference and where it won't. Be cautious if everything you suggest is "no problem".

## Can they show real work?

Ask to see examples of completed projects — ideally similar to yours. Look closely at the details:

- Are joints tight and lines straight?
- Do trims meet neatly at corners?
- Are gaps around doors and cabinetry consistent?
- Does the new work suit the rest of the house?

Photos should be of their own projects. It's reasonable to ask whether a past client would be happy to talk to you.

## Is the scope clear and in writing?

Before work starts, you should know exactly what is included, what isn't, who supplies what and how changes are handled. Clear scope protects both of you and is a sign of a professional operator.

## Who does the regulated work?

Many renovations need plumbing and electrical work, which must be carried out by licensed practitioners. Larger or structural work may need a building permit and, depending on the scope and value, a registered builder. Ask directly:

- Which parts of this job are you doing yourself?
- Which trades will be involved, and are they licensed for that work?
- Does this project need a permit, and who arranges it?

A good renovator will answer these questions plainly. You can check registrations and licences with the relevant Victorian regulator.

## Who will actually be on site?

Some businesses sell the job and send different crews. Others have the owner on the tools. Neither is automatically right or wrong, but you should know who will be doing the work and who you'll talk to when questions come up. Many homeowners value dealing with one experienced person throughout.

## Does their workload suit your project?

Ask how many projects they typically run at once and when they could realistically start. A carpenter who takes on one major renovation at a time may have a longer wait, but can usually give your project more attention.

## Do you feel comfortable with them?

Renovations involve someone working in your home, sometimes for weeks. Trust, communication and respect for your home matter. If something feels off in the first conversations, it rarely improves later.

## Questions worth asking

- Can you work from my plans, or help me develop the idea?
- What would you do differently in this space, and why?
- What will the process look like, step by step?
- How do you handle variations or unexpected issues?
- How will we communicate during the project?

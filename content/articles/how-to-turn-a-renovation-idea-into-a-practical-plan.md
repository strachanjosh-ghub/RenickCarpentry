Many renovations start with a feeling rather than a drawing: "this room never gets used", "I wish the kitchen opened to the garden", "the bathroom is tired". That's a perfectly good place to start. Here's how to turn an idea into something practical.

## 1. Describe the problem, not just the solution

Instead of "I want an island bench", try "there's nowhere for the kids to sit while I cook, and we never have enough bench space". Describing the problem leaves room for better solutions you may not have considered.

## 2. Picture the outcome

How should the finished space feel and work? Brighter? More connected to the garden? Easier to clean? Somewhere to host family? Writing down two or three outcomes helps everyone involved make decisions that serve them.

## 3. Collect inspiration — with notes

Save photos of spaces you like, but add a note about *what* you like in each one: the colour of the timber, the way the light comes in, the open shelving. This is far more useful than the image alone.

## 4. Have a rough budget range in mind

You don't need to share it if you'd rather not, but having a range in mind helps you prioritise. An experienced renovator can explain where money makes the biggest difference and where it doesn't.

## 5. Test the idea against the house

This is where experienced advice is most valuable. Some ideas are straightforward; others run into structure, plumbing, drainage, permits or simply the shape of the house. A good renovator will look at the home itself and tell you honestly what's practical, what may be costly, and what alternatives might achieve the same outcome.

## 6. Identify who else is needed

Depending on the project, you may need a designer, draftsperson, structural engineer, building surveyor or licensed trades. Knowing this early avoids surprises and helps you plan timing.

## 7. Define the scope

A clear scope sets out what will be done, what's included and excluded, who supplies what, and how changes will be handled. This is the point where an idea becomes a plan.

## 8. Decide what matters most

Most projects involve trade-offs. Rank your priorities so that, if something has to give, you know what to protect.

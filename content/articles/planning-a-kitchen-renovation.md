A kitchen renovation involves more decisions than almost any other room — and many of them depend on each other. Making them in the right order is the single best way to avoid rework, delays and extra cost.

## 1. How does your household use the kitchen?

Think about who cooks, how often, and how. Do two people cook at once? Do children do homework at the bench? Is this where guests gather? Do you bake, entertain, or mostly cook quick weeknight meals? These answers decide how much bench space you need, where seating goes and how the kitchen connects to living areas.

## 2. Settle the layout

Common layouts include galley, L-shape, U-shape and island kitchens. The right one depends on the room, the flow of traffic through it and how you cook. Think about the path between fridge, sink and cooktop, and make sure people walking through the kitchen don't cut across the cook's working area.

## 3. Choose appliances early

This is the decision most often left too late. Appliance sizes and types — integrated or freestanding fridge, wall oven or upright cooker, rangehood type, dishwasher — affect cabinet design directly. Choose specific models before cabinetry is finalised, and check their installation requirements.

## 4. Lock in plumbing, power and lighting positions

Sink and dishwasher locations, gas or induction, power points along benches and in the pantry, under-cabinet lighting, pendant positions — these are hard and costly to change once walls are closed and cabinets installed. Plumbing, gas and electrical work must be carried out by licensed trades.

## 5. Decide on any wall or structural changes

Opening up a kitchen to the living area can transform a home, but removing or altering walls may require engineering advice and a building permit. Understand this before committing to a design.

## 6. Design storage around what you own

Rather than generic cabinetry, think about what needs to be stored: pots and pans in deep drawers, a pantry for dry goods, tall cupboards for brooms, space for small appliances so benches stay clear. Good storage is what makes a kitchen feel calm.

## 7. Choose finishes that suit the house

In an established home, door profiles, handles, benchtop materials and colours that relate to the rest of the house help the kitchen feel like it belongs. Consider durability too — some surfaces mark, stain or scratch more easily than others.

## 8. Coordinate everyone involved

A kitchen may involve a designer, cabinet maker, stone fabricator, plumber, electrician, tiler and painter. Each relies on the work before it being accurate. Having someone who understands how all the pieces fit together — and who checks measurements and junctions carefully — makes a big difference to the finished result.

Two renovations can use the same materials and the same layout, yet one feels beautifully finished and the other looks "nearly right". The difference is almost always in the details. Here's what quality looks like, and how to spot it.

## Straight, plumb and level

Lines that should be straight are straight. Door frames are plumb, benchtops are level, shelves don't sag. It sounds basic, but in older homes where nothing is perfectly square, achieving this takes skill and patience.

## Consistent gaps

Look at the gaps around cabinet doors, drawer fronts and internal doors. In a well-finished job they are even and consistent. Uneven gaps are one of the quickest signs of rushed work.

## Tight joints and clean mitres

Where two pieces of timber meet — at the corner of an architrave, a skirting board or a shelf — the joint should be tight with no visible gaps. Mitres should close neatly, and they should stay closed as the seasons change.

## Clean junctions between materials

Renovations are full of junctions: tile to timber, benchtop to splashback, cabinetry to wall, deck to house. Quality work resolves these deliberately, with neat shadow lines, appropriate trims or careful sealing, rather than leaving a messy bead of filler to hide the problem.

## Scribing to real walls

Walls and floors are rarely perfectly straight. Scribing means shaping a panel, benchtop or trim to follow the actual surface, so it sits tight without a visible gap. It takes time, and it's one of the clearest marks of a carpenter who cares about the finish.

## Finishes applied properly

Paint, oil and lacquer only look as good as the preparation underneath. Good finishing includes filling, sanding and priming properly, and protecting surfaces during the rest of the work.

## Materials that suit the use

Quality isn't only about appearance. A beautiful material that wears badly in a busy kitchen or a salty coastal deck isn't a quality choice. Good advice about materials — and honest discussion of maintenance — is part of a quality outcome.

## The bits you don't see

Some of the most important quality is hidden: framing that is straight and solid, waterproofing done properly, fixings suited to the environment, and backing installed in walls for future grab rails, shelves or TV mounts. Ask how these are handled.

## How to judge it

When looking at a renovator's past work, get close. Check corners, gaps, junctions and edges. Open cupboards and drawers. Look at how new work meets old. These details tell you far more than a wide photo ever will.
